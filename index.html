      Hunger drive: does a light day lead to a heavier one? exploratory

      This is an exploratory analysis, not a validated clinical measure; use it for curiosity, not decisions. Each day is compared with the average of its neighbours (three days either side) so growth doesn't masquerade as correlation. The "residual" is ounces above or below that local trend. A lag-1 correlation more negative than shuffled days produce means she compensates (light day, then heavier day). One more positive means light days cluster, which usually points to illness or teething rather than appetite.

      How to read this section
      Trend
      What the surrounding week predicts for a day: the average of the three days before and the three after, with the day itself left out. It tracks growth and slow changes so they don't get mistaken for day-to-day behaviour.
      Residual, "vs trend"
      How far a day's ounces landed from its trend. +2.0 means two ounces more than the surrounding week predicted; −2.0 means two fewer.
      Light / typical / heavy day
      The lightest, middle and heaviest third of days by residual, so each group has the same number of days.
      Correlation (r)
      From −1 to +1. Negative: a low day tends to be followed by a high one (catch-up). Positive: low days follow low days (runs). Near zero: tomorrow is unrelated to today.
      Shuffled days / expected by chance
      The same calculation run 400 times with the days in random order. It is not zero, because comparing each day to its neighbours builds in a small negative correlation on its own. The bar is only meaningful where it escapes this gray range.
      p
      The chance of seeing a gap at least this large between the real result and the shuffled ones if nothing were going on. Below 0.05 is the usual bar for "probably real".
      Slope
      How many ounces tomorrow moves for each ounce today is off trend. −0.5 would mean she makes up half of any shortfall the next day.

      Events & before/after comparison

      Add things that happened: a prescription, the first day of daycare, a cold. They're drawn on every chart and compared below. Events are saved in this browser; export to keep a copy in the folder.

      Before vs. after each event

      "Before" is the window ending the day before the event; "during" is the event's own days (ranges only); "after" is the window starting the day after it ends (or on the event day for single-day events). Partial and excluded days are skipped. Hover a row for a plain-language reading.

      How to read this section
      Before / after
      Average ounces per day in the 7 (or 14) full days on either side of the event. A single-day event's "after" window starts on the event day itself.
      During
      For events with an end date, the average over the event's own days, shown as a diamond in the event's colour.
      Change
      After minus before. Judge it against her normal day-to-day swing (see the hunger-drive card): a one-ounce shift is small when individual days routinely differ by three or four.
      p
      The chance that two windows this different would arise if nothing had changed (a Welch t-test on the daily totals). Below 0.05 is the usual bar for "probably real". With only 7 days a side, only large shifts clear it.
      Caution
      Before/after cannot separate the event from everything else happening at the same time (growth, a cold, a schedule change). It describes what happened; it does not prove the event caused it.
